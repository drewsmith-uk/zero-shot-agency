    Day 102: Marketing Cannot Fix Every AI Visibility Gap

    A GEO report can create a long list of problems that marketing cannot actually solve.

    The company is absent for an important buyer question. The offer is described with an old boundary. A buyer is pointed towards software when the firm sells advisory work. A current service is hard to access. A sales objection keeps appearing in answer-led research. A page that should explain the difference is thin, blocked, stale, or written for the wrong buyer.

    All of those findings may arrive on the CMO's desk because marketing commissioned the visibility work.

    That does not mean marketing owns the correction.

    For CMOs, Marketing Directors, and founders, this is where many AI visibility programmes slow down. The report is treated as a marketing backlog, even when the gap belongs to product truth, commercial policy, delivery capacity, live sales language, technical access, or founder-level positioning. Marketing can observe the gap. It can explain the gap. It can often improve the public explanation around the gap.

    But it cannot unilaterally change the underlying business reality.

    The practical question is not only, “What did the answer say?”

    It is: “Who has authority to make that answer less wrong, less ambiguous, or less commercially damaging?”

    The visibility finding is only the start

    Imagine a specialist B2B firm running a bounded AI visibility check for one priority offer. The team records the surface, prompt, date, market, access context, visible sources where available, and the exact buyer question being tested.

    One generalised finding appears repeatedly enough to deserve attention:

    For a buyer asking who can help diagnose whether AI answers are sending prospects towards the wrong type of provider, the company is sometimes described as a content agency and sometimes omitted in favour of monitoring tools.

    That finding is useful. It is not yet a marketing task.

    Maybe the public site still describes the work in content-production language, so answer-led systems have little reason to understand the diagnostic as a commercial strategy service. Marketing can probably improve that explanation.

    Maybe the actual offer is unclear inside the business. Sales sells a diagnostic, delivery behaves like bespoke advisory, and leadership has not decided whether monitoring tools are competitors, inputs, partners, or disqualifying substitutes. Marketing cannot write its way around that ambiguity.

    Maybe sales is hearing the same objection live: “Can our current agency do this?” In that case, the correction needs a sales-safe response and qualification question, not only a page rewrite.

    Maybe the priority page exists but is not prominent, current, or easy for search and answer systems to reach. That may require web or SEO operations before the explanation can do its job.

    The same observed answer can therefore expose different change surfaces. Treating all of them as “marketing needs more content” is how a visibility report becomes busywork.

    Map the change surface before assigning work

    A compact handoff map is enough. It should not become a ceremonial ownership chart. The point is to identify who can change the condition that produced, permitted, or failed to correct the finding.

    Change surface What the finding sounds like Function with change authority Marketing's real role
    Discovery and category language The company is absent, miscategorised, or placed beside the wrong provider type. Marketing or product marketing when the category and offer are already true; leadership when the category choice is unresolved. Translate the buyer question into clearer public language.
    Public explanation The answer has the right general idea but misses scope, fit boundaries, comparison language, or the next step. Marketing, content, brand, or founder narrative depending on the claim. Improve the owned explanation and make the accurate boundary easier to interpret.
    Offer or product truth The answer describes an obsolete package, unavailable service, wrong pricing model, unrealistic delivery promise, or offer the company no longer wants to grow. Founder, product, commercial leadership, or delivery leadership. Do not launder uncertainty into copy; request the current truth and allowed boundary.
    Live buyer objection The claim appears in sales conversations as a concern about geography, service model, capacity, risk, or fit. Sales leadership with support from marketing; escalate when the issue touches real policy. Provide evidence-safe language, pages, and listening prompts.
    Technical accessibility The right page exists but cannot be crawled, indexed, reached, internally discovered, or understood in context. Web, engineering, SEO, or site operations. Name the commercial consequence and target page.

    The map stops a finding from arriving as an orphaned instruction: “fix AI visibility.” Instead, it arrives as a bounded observation with a named change surface: the page needs clearer wording, the offer boundary needs a business answer, the sales team needs an approved response, the site needs technical repair, or leadership needs to decide whether the demand is desirable.

    Those are different jobs.

    Marketing should not own truth it cannot change

    The most dangerous version of this failure is subtle. Marketing sees the gap, wants to be helpful, and rewrites around an unresolved truth.

    If delivery capacity is constrained, copy may imply availability the team cannot sustain. If a legacy offer is no longer strategic, a page refresh may keep it alive. If pricing or package boundaries are undecided, broad language can make sales conversations harder. If technical access is broken, the team may polish copy that answer systems and search systems cannot reliably reach.

    A healthier rule is stricter: marketing can own expression, not unapproved reality.

    If the finding depends on what the offer is, who it is for, where it is available, how it is delivered, or which demand the business wants more of, authority sits with the function that controls that condition. Marketing can turn the answer into a precise question, show the commercial consequence, draft options, and maintain the public source once the truth is settled.

    But it should not invent the truth to close a visibility ticket.

    Use one finding to create several possible handoffs

    Return to the generalised finding: the company is sometimes treated as a content agency and sometimes displaced by monitoring tools.

    A weak response says: “write more GEO content.”

    A better response splits the finding into tests of authority. If the public explanation is true but weak, marketing can improve the priority page, comparison language, fit/no-fit cues, and next step. If the company has not decided how the diagnostic differs from monitoring software, leadership and product marketing need to define the boundary before marketing publishes a stronger claim. If sales hears buyers asking whether their existing agency can handle the work, sales needs a short qualification pattern. If the strongest explanatory page is not accessible, internally linked, current, or connected to the priority offer, web or SEO operations need to repair the source path.

    If the answer is isolated, old, or surface-specific, the right owner may be nobody yet. The finding can remain a watch note until repeated observations, sales language, or commercial stakes justify action.

    That last option matters. A change-authority map is not a machine for creating work. It is also a way to say: this observation is bounded, low-confidence, or not commercially material enough to disturb the business.

    Keep the evidence limits attached to the handoff

    The handoff should preserve the limits of the observation.

    An answer-led finding does not prove buyer behaviour. It does not prove that a buyer saw the answer, believed it, or changed purchasing direction because of it. It does not prove platform-wide consensus. It does not prove that every absence or misdescription deserves intervention.

    The useful claim is narrower:

    Under these recorded conditions, this surface described or omitted the company in a way that could affect how a relevant buyer understands the route, offer, objection, or next step.

    That sentence is strong enough for an operating conversation and restrained enough to avoid panic.

    The same restraint applies to Google. Google's AI features rely on core Search ranking and quality systems. If a Google AI feature exposes a gap, the response should be grounded in the usefulness, relevance, clarity, accessibility, and quality of the underlying public material. Do not treat llms.txt, special AI markup, arbitrary chunking, or over-focused structured data as required switches for Google AI visibility.

    A good handoff therefore includes five fields: the observed answer or absence, with surface, prompt, date, market, and access limits; the buyer consequence that might matter; the change surface exposed; the function with authority to change the underlying condition; and the smallest next step: clarify, enable, repair, decide, retest, or wait.

    That is enough to prevent the finding from becoming a vague marketing request.

    The leadership question

    The weak question is:

    What should marketing do about the visibility report?

    The stronger question is:

    Which AI visibility gaps can marketing correct through public explanation, and which require authority from sales, product, delivery, web, or leadership because the underlying condition is not a messaging problem?

    That question changes the programme.

    It stops marketing from becoming the dumping ground for every absence, miscategorisation, obsolete offer, sales objection, and access problem. It gives the CMO a way to protect the team's remit without slowing down necessary change. It gives founders a clearer view of which findings are really strategy, operations, sales, or technical work wearing a marketing label.

    AI visibility is not a single-channel communications problem.

    It is a cross-functional change system. Marketing often sees the gap first because it is closest to the public market. The correction belongs wherever the business truth can actually be changed.

    Assign that authority before you buy a larger programme.
