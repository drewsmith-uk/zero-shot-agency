    Day 160: Plan the Whole Commercial Surface Around an AI Overview

    A Google search can now place several commercial opportunities around the same buyer question.

    The AI Overview can explain the subject. Supporting links can open useful web material. Ads can appear above, below or, in some markets and conditions, within the AI Overview.[1]

    For a CMO or Marketing Director, this is one interface with distinct jobs. Content can serve the research; paid search can offer a relevant next step. The teams should brief the surface together.

    The anatomy below is schematic. It is not a captured result, a promised layout or a campaign outcome.

    Start with the question entering the interface

    Imagine a facilities leader searches:

    How should I compare workplace air-quality monitors across several offices?

    The question is hypothetical. No Google response, source selection, advert or auction result was observed for this post.

    It gives both teams a shared situation: a buyer is learning the comparison criteria before choosing what to inspect next. Map the possible surface around that task.

    Surface one: the answer frames the decision

    The AI Overview occupies the explanatory layer. Here, a useful answer would frame what is measured, where monitoring occurs, how readings are interpreted and what deserves verification.

    Google says AI Overviews help people get the gist of a complicated question and provide a jumping-off point for further exploration.[2] That description does not guarantee that this particular query will trigger one, or that any named company will appear.

    The content commission should begin with the research need: definitions, decision criteria, limitations, implementation conditions and checkable evidence. Do not force a product mention into every paragraph.

    Surface two: supporting links open the evidence routes

    Google says AI Overviews can surface links that help people explore supporting web material.[2] A page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet to be eligible as a supporting link; meeting those conditions does not guarantee that Google will crawl, index or serve it.[2]

    One link might explain placement and calibration questions; another might lead to an independent technical standard. A product page may matter later, when the buyer needs specifications.

    The organic job is depth: provide material worth opening when a short synthesis cannot carry the full decision.

    No special AI file or markup is required for this eligibility. Google says ordinary Search foundations remain relevant and that AI Overviews and AI Mode have no additional technical requirements.[2]

    Surface three: the advert offers a next step

    Google documents that ads can be eligible to appear above, below or within AI Overviews. For ads within an AI Overview, Google says serving considers both the user's query and the Overview's content; existing eligible Search, Shopping and Performance Max ads may show when the relevant conditions are met.[1]

    Paid creative has a different job from the guide: movement.

    An advert might offer a comparison tool, a bounded site-assessment call or a product-selection worksheet. It should connect the research context to a specific action, without imitating the answer or claiming its recommendation.

    Availability and placement are conditional. Google's current documentation limits ads within AI Overviews by language, country, device, campaign eligibility and vertical; ads above or below have broader availability.[1] Verify the current market rules rather than treating this anatomy as an inventory promise.

    Brief the two jobs together

    Put one page in front of the paid-search and content leads. At the top, write the buyer question. Beneath it, divide the commission into two outputs:

    • Useful organic material: the part of the buyer's research that deserves a full, checkable explanation.
    • Relevant paid next step: the action the company can credibly offer when the buyer is ready to move from understanding to evaluation.

    Then ask whether the pair makes sense in the same search experience. Does the content deepen the decision? Does the advert advance it? Are both written for the same buyer state without pretending that payment determines the answer or its supporting links?

    Answer-led search is becoming a mixed commercial interface. Plan for the whole surface: help the buyer understand, give them a worthwhile route into the evidence, and make the paid next step useful when it appears.

    [1] Google Ads Help, “About ads and AI Overviews”: https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775?hl=en

    [2] Google Search Central, “AI features and your website”: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features
