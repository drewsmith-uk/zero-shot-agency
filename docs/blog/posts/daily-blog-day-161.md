# Day 161: Map the Places That Make a Service Local

A field-service company may have one headquarters, three engineer routes and customers asking for help at dozens of sites.

Which place should shape its discovery brief?

The answer is rarely “where the logo is registered”. Local discovery is situated around a buyer who needs a particular service at a particular place. Marketing therefore needs to distinguish the company’s genuine operating presence, the area it can actually serve and the site where the buyer needs the work.

The annotated map below is a planning exercise for a fictional commercial refrigeration business. It is not a client case, a live search test or a report of observed rankings.

## Draw the service geography before choosing the pages

Imagine Northstar Cooling, a fictional company with one genuine operating base and mobile engineers serving commercial sites.

**A — Operating base**

This is the real staffed location from which engineers and equipment are dispatched. It is an operating fact, not a claim that buyers visit it.

**B — Regular service area**

Here Northstar provides planned maintenance and emergency callouts under its normal conditions. The boundary reflects delivery capacity, not a radius added to appear more local.

**C — Buyer Site One: distribution depot**

The depot sits inside the service area and needs emergency repair. Its facilities manager is asking whether an engineer can serve this site, for this fault, under the required conditions.

**D — Buyer Site Two: restaurant group head office**

The procurement team works inside the area, but several restaurants sit outside it. Its office does not make those operating sites serviceable.

**E — Buyer Site Three: planned expansion**

A depot beyond the boundary may become serviceable after a new engineer route exists. Today it is a future decision, not a present claim.

These five annotations produce a better commissioning question: which service-and-site combinations can the company truthfully support now?

## Give each geographic layer its proper job

Google says local results in Search and Maps are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity. It defines distance in relation to the customer who is searching, using what it knows about that person’s location when no location is supplied.[1]

The searcher may be at a head office rather than the site that needs repair. Google’s description of distance does not promise that results will be centred on the worksite. The worksite matters here as an operational and content-planning constraint: it determines whether Northstar can truthfully serve the buyer’s need.

That documented model makes the searcher’s location relevant to ordinary local Search. It does not say headquarters is irrelevant, nor does it give marketers a way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Google also distinguishes storefront, service-area and hybrid businesses. Its service-area guidance says the service area shows customers where a business can provide products and services. A business that does not serve customers at its address should remove that address from its profile, and service areas are specified by places such as cities or postal codes rather than an editable radius.[2] Google’s representation guidance says a business can create a profile when customers can visit its physical location or when it travels to customers.[3]

Those profile fields should describe the operating model. They do not manufacture premises, extend capacity or prove that every request can be accepted.

## Commission one useful route through the map

For Northstar, the immediate commercial choice is to support Buyer Site One’s task.

The brief is not “publish a page for every town”. It is one useful route explaining emergency refrigeration repair for commercial depots within the company’s genuine service coverage. The page should state the equipment and faults covered, the response conditions, the information needed at first contact, material exclusions and how a facilities manager confirms whether a site can be served.

The Business Profile and website should describe the same operating reality. Sales should not quote Buyer Site Three as covered until delivery capacity actually changes. The restaurant-group enquiry should be assessed against each operating site, rather than the postcode of its head office.

## Keep the AI-assisted question bounded

Google says its AI Overviews and AI Mode use the same foundational SEO practices as Search, with no extra technical requirements or special AI markup. It also says supporting links may be surfaced and that inclusion is not guaranteed.[4]

That supports a careful planning question: if a buyer uses Google to explore a location-specific service need, does the company publish accurate, useful information about the service and the places it can genuinely cover?

It does not establish that local ranking factors form a complete AI recommendation model. It does not predict which page or Business Profile will appear, and it says nothing about recommendation behaviour in ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity or Gemini.

The map still improves the marketing decision. Start with the buyer’s worksite, connect it to a service the company can deliver, and represent the operating geography truthfully. Invest where those three points meet.
